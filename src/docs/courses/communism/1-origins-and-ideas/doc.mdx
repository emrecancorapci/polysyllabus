Communism is a family of political and economic ideas holding that the means of production should be owned in common rather than privately. As a *theory* it belongs to political philosophy; as a *movement* it shaped the twentieth century. This lesson stays on the theory.

## Where it came from

Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels published **The Communist Manifesto** in 1848, a year of revolutions across Europe. It distilled ideas already in circulation — socialist critiques of industrial capitalism, German philosophy, French political thought — into a programmatic text.

<Callout variant="note" title="Primary source">
  The Manifesto opens with one of the most quoted lines in political writing: "A spectre is haunting Europe — the spectre of communism." Reading the first two sections is the fastest way into the argument.
</Callout>

## Core concepts

### Class struggle

Marx's starting point is that history is driven by conflict between classes defined by their relationship to production. In capitalism, the central conflict runs between:

- the **bourgeoisie** — owners of capital and the means of production, and
- the **proletariat** — those who must sell their labor to survive.

### Historical materialism

The material conditions of an era (how goods are produced, who owns what) shape its politics, law, and ideas. Institutions and ideologies are explained as outgrowths of the economic base, not as independent forces.

### The critique of capitalism

The Manifesto argues that capitalism, while historically productive, contains structural tensions: it concentrates wealth, alienates workers from their labor, and produces recurring crises. Marx expected these tensions to lead the working class to replace the system.

## Theory versus practice

Later states that ruled *in communism's name* — the Soviet Union most influentially — made choices Marx never specified: one-party rule, centralized planning, suppression of dissent. Evaluating the theory separately from these implementations is a basic move in the literature.

  In Marx's model of capitalism, name the two central classes and what distinguishes them. The **bourgeoisie** own the means of production; the **proletariat** own only their labor, which they sell for wages.
</CollapsibleSolution>

## Key takeaways

- Communism theorizes common ownership of the means of production.
- Marx and Engels ground it in class struggle and historical materialism.
- The critique of capitalism targets concentration of wealth, alienation, and crises.
- Twentieth-century communist states are best analyzed as distinct from the theory they invoked.
