Ohm's law describes one element. Kirchhoff's laws describe how the whole circuit hangs together, and they follow directly from two conservation principles.

## Charge conservation at a node

Charge cannot pile up at an ideal node. The currents flowing **into** a node must equal the currents flowing **out**:

$$
\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}
$$

  ![A circuit node where current I1 flows in and currents I2 and I3 flow out](./fig-kcl-node.svg)
</Figure>

## Using the law

If $I_1 = 3\,\text{A}$ and $I_2 = 1.2\,\text{A}$, then KCL fixes the last branch immediately: $I_3 = 3 - 1.2 = 1.8\,\text{A}$.

## Key takeaways

- KCL: charge conservation at a node, $\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$.
- Any single unknown branch current at a node follows from the others.
