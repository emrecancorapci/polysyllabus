## Reading the two laws together

The two laws divide the work: KCL constrains currents at junctions, KVL constrains voltages along paths.

    A --> C["KVL around loops<br/>sums of element voltages"]

## Worked example

A 9 V source drives a series pair: $R_1 = 100\,\Omega$ and $R_2 = 200\,\Omega$.

1. **Ohm's law for the series combination**: the same current flows through both resistors, so $I = 9\,\text{V} / 300\,\Omega = 30\,\text{mA}$.
2. **Voltage drops**: $V_1 = I \, R_1 = 3\,\text{V}$ and $V_2 = I \, R_2 = 6\,\text{V}$.
3. **KVL check**: around the loop, $-9 + 3 + 6 = 0$. ✓

The check step is the habit to build: every loop calculation should close back to zero.

## Key takeaways

- Ohm's law handles single elements, and Kirchhoff's laws handle the connections.
- Together they are enough to analyze any lumped circuit.
- Always close the loop with a KVL check.
