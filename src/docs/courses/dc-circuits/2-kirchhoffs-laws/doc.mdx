Ohm's law describes one element. Kirchhoff's laws describe how the whole circuit hangs together — and they follow directly from two conservation principles.

## Kirchhoff's current law (KCL)

Charge cannot pile up at an ideal node. The currents flowing **into** a node must equal the currents flowing **out**:

$$
\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}
$$

The figure shows a node with one incoming and two outgoing branches:

![A circuit node where current I1 flows in and currents I2 and I3 flow out](./fig-kcl-node.svg)

If $I_1 = 3\,\text{A}$ and $I_2 = 1.2\,\text{A}$, then KCL fixes the last branch immediately: $I_3 = 3 - 1.2 = 1.8\,\text{A}$.

## Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL)

Energy is conserved around any closed loop: the voltages raised by sources equal the voltages dropped across loads.

$$
\sum_{\text{loop}} V = 0
$$

The sign convention takes a voltage as you pass its polarity: rises positive, drops negative — or the reverse, as long as you are consistent around the loop.

## Worked example

A 9 V source drives a series pair: $R_1 = 100\,\Omega$ and $R_2 = 200\,\Omega$.

1. **Ohm's law for the series combination**: the same current flows through both resistors, so $I = 9\,\text{V} / 300\,\Omega = 30\,\text{mA}$.
2. **Voltage drops**: $V_1 = I \, R_1 = 3\,\text{V}$ and $V_2 = I \, R_2 = 6\,\text{V}$.
3. **KVL check**: around the loop, $-9 + 3 + 6 = 0$. ✓

The check step is the habit to build: every loop calculation should close back to zero.

## Key takeaways

- KCL: charge conservation at a node, $\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$.
- KVL: energy conservation around a loop, $\sum V = 0$, with a consistent sign convention.
- Ohm's law handles single elements; Kirchhoff's laws handle the connections. Together they are enough to analyze any lumped circuit.
